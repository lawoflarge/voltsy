Where the alternatives beat Voltsy

Settings wins on the actual health figure

Maximum capacity, and on iPhone 15 and later the cycle count, the manufacture date and the first use date. Voltsy cannot show any of that and does not pretend to. It links you to Settings from inside the app.

Desktop tools win on depth

Plugged into a Mac or PC, an iPhone exposes diagnostic data no on device app can reach. If you are buying a used iPhone, run coconutBattery or iMazing before you hand over money. No App Store app can substitute for that.

Apple's Batteries widget wins on the plain number

It is not subject to the five percent rounding that third party apps get, and it covers paired accessories. Voltsy's widget shows Volt, not a figure.

Charge Limit wins over any reminder

On iPhone 15 and later, a ceiling in Settings stops the charge without your attention. A notification cannot compete with that. Use the setting if you have it.

Where Voltsy is different

Voltsy does not compete on numbers, because on iOS that competition is decided before it starts. It competes on whether you actually change what you do.

  • A character whose mood you read in a second, rather than a table you stop opening after a week.
  • A nudge at the moment the charge crosses your threshold, which is when the decision is live.
  • A daily streak with a freeze token, so one bad day does not make you quit.
  • Figures that stay blank until there is enough data to be honest, including a Battery Care score that appears only after about three observed cycles.

How to spot an app that is guessing

If you are comparing battery apps in the App Store, these are the tells.

  • It shows a battery health percentage or a cycle count. iOS does not provide either to apps, so the figure is derived, not measured.
  • It reports a battery temperature in degrees. Apps get four coarse thermal levels, not a reading.
  • It offers to close background apps or free memory to save battery. iOS does not allow that.
  • It changes its charge percentage in single points on iOS 17 or later. Apps receive the level in roughly five percent steps.
